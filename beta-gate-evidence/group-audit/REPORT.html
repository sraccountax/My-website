What worked

    Every menu, both editions, both themes

    Each sweep signs in, sets the mode, theme and navigation through the real Preferences page, then opens every menu item, every report and every settings page, and the first "New/Add/Create" form on each. It records script errors, failed requests, sideways overflow, controls off screen, "undefined" or NaN text, missing headings and wording from the other edition.

    The theme, reviewed as a UI designer

    Tegh's identity today is a dark green sidebar, a teal action colour and a champagne-gold accent, with a separate navy-and-teal sign-in page.

    MeasuredResultWhy it matters
    Brand colour#0f766eThis is exactly Tailwind's default teal-700. Most accounting tools already use green, teal or blue, so Tegh reads as one more of them.
    Brand vs "paid/success" greenΔE 4.5Perceptual distance (OKLab ×100). Under about 15 people cannot tell two colours apart at a glance, so a Paid chip and a primary button look the same.
    Different hex colours in the CSS2,5485,433 literal colour uses across 30 stylesheets. Changing a few tokens does not re-theme the product; most colours are typed in place.
    Core tokens defined more than onceaccent ×3 · muted ×7 · danger ×6 · success ×5The same token name means different colours on different screens, which is why some pages look slightly off-brand.
    Contrast today5.5:1 button · 4.8:1 muted textPasses WCAG AA. Any new palette must keep at least this.

    Three proposals, rendered on the real app

    Each proposal was applied to the running app by remapping every brand colour in the stylesheets in the browser (OKLCH, keeping each colour's lightness so contrast stays as designed). Status colours for paid, overdue and errors were left alone. Nothing in the product was changed.

    Recommendation

    Choose Aubergine & Brass. It is the only one of the three that is unusual among accounting products, keeps every contrast pair above today's, and sits far from the status colours (ΔE 19.7 from success, 12.7 from danger). Brass ties to the existing champagne accent, so the logo and marketing pages carry over with a colour change rather than a redesign.

    Ink & Marigold is the stronger fallback if the brand should feel more "professional tool" than "boutique". Avoid Terracotta & Slate unless the warning and error colours are redesigned with it: it lands ΔE 5.6 from warning orange.

    What to do next

    Group bookkeeping

    1. Fix the three defects first. Show the minus sign on the dashboard cash figure; stop defaulting provincial HST on bills from suppliers outside Canada; refuse India fields on a Canada-edition company request instead of creating a Canadian company.
    2. Mark related parties. Let a customer or vendor be linked to another company in the same group, with a due-to/due-from account on each side.
    3. Mirror documents. An invoice to a linked customer creates a draft bill in the other company (same number, date, amount, currency); a payment does the same. The other side approves the draft.
    4. Intercompany reconciliation report. Pairs each company's due-to/due-from and IC revenue/expense, as at a date, and shows differences like the $450 interest error.
    5. Consolidation workspace. Eliminates matched IC balances and IC revenue/expense, translates other base currencies (closing rate for the balance sheet, average for P&L, difference to a translation reserve), and treats an associate by the equity method.
    6. Group close and one sign-in for the whole group. Close a period for every selected company at once; show Canadian and India companies in one switcher, each still following its own edition.
    7. India cross-border. Section 195 TDS for payments to non-residents, and GST reverse charge on imported services (GSTR-3B already says these boxes are not computed).

    Theme

    1. Consolidate tokens before changing colours. One tokens file with semantic names (--brand, --brand-strong, --surface, --ink-muted, --status-paid, --status-overdue …), each defined once per theme.
    2. Replace literal colours with tokens, starting with the 25 most used (842 of the 5,433 uses), and add a lint rule that rejects new hex values outside the tokens file.
    3. Separate status from brand. Paid/positive stays green, overdue red, due-soon amber, each with an icon and a word so colour is never the only signal.
    4. Apply the chosen scheme to the app, sign-in, documents (invoice templates keep the customer's own colours) and marketing site, then recolour the mark.
    5. Tidy the number display: show the currency once per report instead of in every cell, show zero months as a dash, never wrap a minus sign away from its figure.

    How this was tested, and its limits

    • Where: the local gate host running the R198 package, with a faked clock of 3 October 2026. Nothing was run against IONOS or books-test, and no existing company was changed.
    • Group test: a script that creates the four companies through the same API the browser uses, posts every transaction, then reads the ledgers directly to check balances. Three harness mistakes were corrected along the way (wrong field names, the payment date after the test clock, a rates call made before the currency was added); the results above are from one clean final run on a fresh group.
    • Screen sweep: Playwright in Chromium, signed in as the group owner, through the real Preferences page.
    • Theme mock-ups: generated, not designed by hand. Some colours inside images and charts are not remapped, and a production version would tune each token rather than shift hues. In the Ink & Marigold sign-in, the small line above the headline turned ink on ink; a designed version would set it in marigold. The contrast and distance figures are computed from the exact colours shown.
    • Not covered: payroll across companies, bank feeds from real banks, and PDF documents from the group (each was exercised in earlier audits on single companies).
