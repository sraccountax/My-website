Accounting Methods

Cash vs accrual accounting

The difference is mainly timing: accrual accounting focuses on when economic activity is earned or incurred, while cash accounting focuses on when cash moves.

Accrual accounting

Revenue is generally recognized when earned and expenses when incurred, even when the related cash is received or paid later. Receivables and payables therefore remain visible on the Balance Sheet.

Cash accounting

Cash-basis reporting focuses on cash received and cash paid. The timing of a customer payment or vendor payment can therefore change when activity appears in a cash-basis report.

A simple example

If a customer invoice is issued in December and paid in January, accrual reporting normally reflects the revenue in December while cash-basis reporting generally reflects the cash receipt in January, subject to the applicable accounting and tax rules.

Why consistency matters

A business should not switch between methods simply to achieve a preferred result. The reporting basis should match the business’s requirements and be applied consistently.
