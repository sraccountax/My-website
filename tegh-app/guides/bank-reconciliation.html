Banking Guide

What is bank reconciliation?

Bank reconciliation compares the accounting balance for a bank account with the bank statement and explains every difference.

Why the balances can differ

Outstanding payments, deposits in transit, bank charges, interest, duplicate entries and missing transactions can all cause the book balance and bank statement balance to differ.

What reconciliation proves

The goal is not simply to force two totals to match. Each difference should have a valid explanation and the accounting records should contain the transactions that belong in the period.

A practical sequence

Confirm the statement period and ending balance, match cleared receipts and payments, identify outstanding items, record genuine bank-only activity, investigate duplicates or omissions, then confirm the adjusted balances agree.

Common warning signs

Old outstanding items, unexplained manual adjustments, repeated duplicates, transactions posted to the wrong bank account and a reconciliation that changes after it was completed all deserve review.

In Tegh

Use Match and Post to review, categorize and match statement lines or existing payments. Reconcile Account compares the imported bank position with the ledger and separates outstanding items. Completion requires an unexplained difference of $0.00; valid reconciling items can remain and are recorded.
