Accounting Guide

How to read a Balance Sheet

A Balance Sheet is a point-in-time view of what a business controls, what it owes and the equity that remains.

Start with the accounting equation

Assets = Liabilities + Equity. A correctly prepared Balance Sheet must satisfy this equation. If it does not, the underlying accounting records need investigation before the report can be relied on.

Read assets by liquidity

Start with cash and bank balances, then receivables and other current assets before moving to longer-term assets such as equipment. Compare liquid resources with obligations that will require cash soon.

Review liabilities by timing

Accounts Payable, taxes payable and other short-term obligations help show near-term cash demands. Long-term loans and other financing explain obligations that extend beyond the current operating cycle.

Understand equity

Equity is the residual interest after liabilities. Changes in retained earnings and current earnings should make sense when compared with the Profit & Loss and prior-period activity.

What to investigate

Look for unusual negative balances, old receivables, large unexplained control-account balances, rapidly changing liabilities and balances that do not agree with supporting ledgers.

In Tegh

Use the Balance Sheet together with Trial Balance, GL Account Ledger, customer/vendor ledgers and Day Book so a reported number can be traced back to its accounting source.
