Receivables Guide

Understanding AR Aging

Accounts Receivable Aging groups unpaid customer balances by how long they have been outstanding so collection risk becomes visible.

Typical aging buckets

Aging reports commonly separate Current balances from 1–30, 31–60, 61–90 and 90+ day balances. The exact buckets matter less than using them consistently.

What older balances mean

A growing older bucket can indicate collection problems, disputes, incorrect allocations or customers paying outside agreed terms even when total sales remain strong.

Do not treat every old balance the same

A disputed invoice, an agreed installment plan and an unapplied customer credit require different follow-up. Notes and customer-level history help explain why a balance remains open.

Reconcile the report

The total AR Aging should agree with the Accounts Receivable control balance in the General Ledger. Differences can indicate direct control-account postings, missing subledger activity or cutover problems.

In Tegh

Use customer ledgers, payment terms, credit and debit notes, and receivable ageing together. Tegh Intelligence ranks overdue customers using amount owed, days late and recent payment habits, and links to Collection Drafts. Historical opening invoices remain available for collection without recreating prior-period revenue.
